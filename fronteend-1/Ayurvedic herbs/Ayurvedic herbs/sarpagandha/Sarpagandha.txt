--- SARPAGANDHA ---

[Intro]
Sarpagandha is a highly potent medicinal herb famous worldwide for its powerful hypotensive (blood-pressure-lowering) and sedative properties.

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Order: Gentianales | Family: Apocynaceae | Genus: Rauvolfia | Species: R. serpentina

[Morphology]
An erect, evergreen shrub reaching about 1 meter in height, with whorled leaves and clusters of white/pink flowers yielding dark purple fruits.

[Habitat and Distribution]
Native to the Indian subcontinent and East Asia; thrives in tropical, moist forests.

[Historical Background]
Used for centuries in India for treating snakebites, severe anxiety, and mania. It became famous in the West in the 1950s as the source of reserpine.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Contains over 50 indole alkaloids, most notably reserpine, rescinnamine, and yohimbine.

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Dramatically lowers high blood pressure, induces sleep (treats insomnia), calms severe anxiety, and treats schizophrenia.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa: Bitter, Pungent | Virya: Heating | Vipaka: Pungent | Dosha: Pacifies Vata and Kapha.

[Methods of Consumption]
Root powder or tablets taken under strict medical supervision. Due to its potency, it is not a DIY herbal remedy.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
Can cause severe depression, nasal congestion, and lethargy if overdosed. Strictly contraindicated during pregnancy, in nursing mothers, and alongside modern blood-pressure medications.
