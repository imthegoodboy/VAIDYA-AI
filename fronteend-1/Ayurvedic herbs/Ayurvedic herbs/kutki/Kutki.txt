--- KUTKI ---

[Intro]
Kutki is a potent, bitter root primarily known in Ayurveda as a premier liver tonic and detoxifying herb.

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Order: Lamiales | Family: Plantaginaceae | Genus: Picrorhiza | Species: P. kurroa

[Morphology]
A small perennial herb with a long, creeping rootstock (rhizome). Leaves are sharply serrated, and it produces pale purple to white flowers.

[Habitat and Distribution]
Native to the rocky, alpine regions of the Himalayas (India, Nepal, Tibet) at elevations of 3,000 to 5,000 meters.

[Historical Background]
Revered in traditional medicine for centuries as an emergency remedy for jaundice, sudden fevers, and snake bites.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Rich in iridoid glycosides (picroside I and II, kutkoside), cucurbitacins, and apocynin.

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Highly hepatoprotective (protects the liver), choleretic (stimulates bile), anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and immune-modulating.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa: Bitter | Virya: Cooling | Vipaka: Pungent | Dosha: Deeply pacifies Pitta and Kapha, but can aggravate Vata if used in excess.

[Methods of Consumption]
Usually taken as a powder mixed with warm water, honey, or ghee, often in small doses due to its intense bitterness.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
May cause diarrhea or abdominal pain in high doses. Should be avoided during pregnancy and by those with severe hypoglycemia.
