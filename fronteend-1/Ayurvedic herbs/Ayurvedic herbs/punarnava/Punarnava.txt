--- PUNARNAVA ---

[Intro]
Punarnava translates to 'that which renews the body'. It is a powerful rejuvenating herb targeting the kidneys and liver.

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Order: Caryophyllales | Family: Nyctaginaceae | Genus: Boerhavia | Species: B. diffusa

[Morphology]
A creeping, spreading perennial weed with deep fleshy roots, purplish stems, and small pink to reddish flowers.

[Habitat and Distribution]
Found throughout India, the Pacific, and the southern United States as a common weed.

[Historical Background]
Used in Ayurveda specifically to manage edema (fluid retention) and to rejuvenate failing organ systems.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Punarnavine (alkaloid), boeravinones, flavonoids, and potassium salts.

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Potent diuretic (without depleting potassium), protects the liver, supports heart health, manages obesity, and treats urinary tract infections.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa: Sweet, Bitter, Astringent | Virya: Heating | Vipaka: Pungent | Dosha: Balances all three doshas, especially Kapha.

[Methods of Consumption]
Root powder, fresh leaf juice, or as a decoction. Often formulated into 'Punarnavadi Guggulu'.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
Generally safe. Those with severe cardiac conditions or those taking prescription diuretics should consult a physician due to its fluid-draining effects.
