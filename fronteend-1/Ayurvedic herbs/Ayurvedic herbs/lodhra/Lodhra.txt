--- LODHRA ---

[Intro]
Lodhra is a significant Ayurvedic herb traditionally used as a uterine tonic and to treat bleeding disorders and skin conditions.

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Order: Ericales | Family: Symplocaceae | Genus: Symplocos | Species: S. racemosa

[Morphology]
An evergreen medium-sized tree with dark green, elliptical leaves. It bears white to yellowish fragrant flowers and purplish-black drupe fruits.

[Habitat and Distribution]
Found throughout India, particularly in the northeastern regions, as well as in Myanmar and parts of Southeast Asia.

[Historical Background]
Mentioned extensively in classic Ayurvedic texts (Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita) as a supreme herb for women's health.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Contains loturine, loturidine, colloturine (alkaloids), glycosides, and phytosterols.

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Acts as an astringent, stops excessive bleeding (hemostatic), supports ovarian health (PCOS), and treats acne and blemishes.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa: Astringent, Bitter | Virya: Cooling | Vipaka: Pungent | Dosha: Pacifies Pitta and Kapha.

[Methods of Consumption]
Bark powder consumed with water, milk, or rice water. Often used as a paste (lepa) for topical application on skin issues.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
Generally safe, but excessive consumption on an empty stomach may cause nausea or constipation due to its heavy astringency.
