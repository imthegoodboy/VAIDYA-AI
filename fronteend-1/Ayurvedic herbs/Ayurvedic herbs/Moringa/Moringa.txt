--- MORINGA ---

[Intro]
Moringa, often called the 'Miracle Tree', is a highly nutrient-dense plant used to combat malnutrition and reduce inflammation.

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Order: Brassicales | Family: Moringaceae | Genus: Moringa | Species: M. oleifera

[Morphology]
A fast-growing, drought-resistant tree with drooping branches, feathery foliage, fragrant white flowers, and long seed pods (drumsticks).

[Habitat and Distribution]
Native to the Indian subcontinent, now cultivated extensively in tropical and subtropical areas worldwide.

[Historical Background]
Used in traditional Indian medicine and diet for thousands of years; historically utilized by armies to sustain stamina.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Extremely high in Vitamin C, Vitamin A, Calcium, Potassium, Iron, complete proteins, flavonoids (quercetin), and glucosinolates.

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Lowers blood sugar, reduces systemic inflammation, lowers cholesterol, protects against arsenic toxicity, and supports lactation.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa: Pungent, Bitter | Virya: Heating | Vipaka: Pungent | Dosha: Pacifies Kapha and Vata; can aggravate Pitta due to its heating nature.

[Methods of Consumption]
Leaves eaten fresh or cooked, dried leaf powder in smoothies/water, and pods cooked in curries.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
Root and bark contain alkaloids that can be toxic. Pregnant women should avoid high doses of moringa root/bark due to potential abortifacient properties.
