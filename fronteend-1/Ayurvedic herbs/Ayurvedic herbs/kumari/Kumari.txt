--- KUMARI ---

[Intro]
Kumari, commonly known as Aloe Vera, is a highly regarded succulent plant known for its soothing, cooling, and healing properties.

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Clade: Tracheophytes | Order: Asparagales | Family: Asphodelaceae | Genus: Aloe | Species: A. vera

[Morphology]
A stemless or very short-stemmed plant with thick, fleshy, green to grey-green leaves. The leaf margins are serrated with small white teeth.

[Habitat and Distribution]
Originated in the Arabian Peninsula, but grows widely in tropical, semi-tropical, and arid climates around the world.

[Historical Background]
Used for over 5,000 years, documented in ancient Egyptian, Greek, and Indian Ayurvedic texts as a panacea for skin and digestive issues.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Contains over 75 active components including vitamins (A, C, E, B12), enzymes (aliiase, amylase), minerals (calcium, magnesium), sugars (acemannan), and anthraquinones (aloin).

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Promotes wound healing, treats burns, reduces dental plaque, relieves constipation, lowers blood sugar levels, and improves skin elasticity.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa (Taste): Bitter, Sweet | Virya (Potency): Cooling | Vipaka (Post-digestive effect): Sweet | Dosha: Balances all three doshas (Vata, Pitta, Kapha), especially effective for pacifying Pitta.

[Methods of Consumption]
Fresh gel applied topically, consumed as juice (with water/honey), or encapsulated as a supplement.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
Oral consumption of aloe latex (aloin) can cause severe cramping and diarrhea. Pregnant women should avoid oral intake as it may stimulate uterine contractions.
