--- NAGKESAR ---

[Intro]
Nagkesar is a fragrant herb heavily utilized in Ayurveda for its hemostatic properties—stopping bleeding—and promoting digestion.

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Order: Malpighiales | Family: Calophyllaceae | Genus: Mesua | Species: M. ferrea

[Morphology]
A medium-to-large evergreen tree with a dense canopy. The flowers are large, white, and highly fragrant, with prominent golden-yellow stamens (the part used medically).

[Habitat and Distribution]
Native to the wet, tropical parts of Sri Lanka, India, southern Nepal, and Southeast Asia.

[Historical Background]
Revered as an ornamental tree in temple gardens; the dried stamens have been used in classical Ayurvedic medicine for centuries.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Mesuanic acid, alpha- and beta-amyrin, beta-sitosterol, and various essential oils.

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Excellent for bleeding piles (hemorrhoids), menorrhagia (heavy periods), treats asthma, relieves fever, and supports wound healing.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa: Astringent, Bitter | Virya: Slightly Heating | Vipaka: Pungent | Dosha: Pacifies Kapha and Pitta.

[Methods of Consumption]
Dried stamen powder mixed with butter, ghee, or honey. Used as an ingredient in the famous formula 'Chyawanprash'.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
Generally very safe, but should be used under guidance during pregnancy. Overdose may cause slight gastric upset.
