--- MUSTA ---

[Intro]
Musta (also known as Nutgrass) is a primary Ayurvedic herb for managing digestive disorders, fever, and female reproductive issues.

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Order: Poales | Family: Cyperaceae | Genus: Cyperus | Species: C. rotundus

[Morphology]
A perennial grass with an extensive underground network of rhizomes and tubers. Stems are triangular, bearing grass-like leaves.

[Habitat and Distribution]
Native to Africa, Southern and Central Europe, and Southern Asia. Often considered an invasive weed in agricultural lands.

[Historical Background]
Highly praised in the Charaka Samhita for its ability to correct metabolic fire (Agni) and manage Pitta-related fevers.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Essential oils (cyperene, patchoulenone), flavonoids, terpenoids, and cyperol.

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Antidiarrheal, anti-spasmodic, supports healthy digestion, relieves menstrual cramps, and acts as an effective anti-inflammatory.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa: Pungent, Bitter, Astringent | Virya: Cooling | Vipaka: Pungent | Dosha: Balances Pitta and Kapha.

[Methods of Consumption]
Tuber powder taken with warm water or honey; often used in decoctions for fevers and diarrhea.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
Generally safe. Persons with severe constipation or Vata aggravation should use it cautiously due to its astringent nature.
