--- MANJISTHA ---

[Intro]
Manjistha is considered one of the best blood-purifying (alterative) herbs in Ayurveda, famous for supporting lymphatic health and clearing the skin.

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Order: Gentianales | Family: Rubiaceae | Genus: Rubia | Species: R. cordifolia

[Morphology]
A climbing or scrambling herb with long, cylindrical, red-colored roots. Leaves are ovate-heart-shaped, and flowers are small, greenish-yellow.

[Habitat and Distribution]
Native to the mountainous regions of India, Nepal, Tibet, and China.

[Historical Background]
Historically used not only as medicine but also as a natural red dye for fabrics due to its strong pigment.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Rich in anthraquinones (purpurin, munjistin), glycosides, and bicyclic hexapeptides.

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Potent detoxifier, supports kidney function, reduces hyperpigmentation, treats acne, and possesses anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa: Bitter, Astringent, Sweet | Virya: Cooling | Vipaka: Pungent | Dosha: Pacifies Pitta and Kapha.

[Methods of Consumption]
Root powder taken with warm water, honey, or ghee. Also widely used in medicated oils (Mahanarayan oil) for topical massage.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
May temporarily turn urine or stool an orange/red color (harmless). Caution during pregnancy and with severe bleeding disorders.
