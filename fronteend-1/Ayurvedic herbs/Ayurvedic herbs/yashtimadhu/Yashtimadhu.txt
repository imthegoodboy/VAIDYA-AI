--- YASHTIMADHU ---

[Intro]
Yashtimadhu translates to 'sweet stick' and is the Ayurvedic name for Licorice. (Note: This shares the exact profile as Licorice).

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Order: Fabales | Family: Fabaceae | Genus: Glycyrrhiza | Species: G. glabra

[Morphology]
Herbaceous perennial with a deep, stoloniferous woody root system that holds its medicinal sweetness.

[Habitat and Distribution]
Native to Western Asia and Southern Europe.

[Historical Background]
Considered a crucial Harmonizing herb in formulations, masking bitter tastes and enhancing the effects of other herbs.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Glycyrrhizin, glabridin, liquiritin.

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Excellent for vocal cord care (singers use it frequently), heals hyperacidity and ulcers, clears throat mucus, and naturally lightens skin pigmentation.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa: Sweet | Virya: Cooling | Vipaka: Sweet | Dosha: Balances Vata and Pitta.

[Methods of Consumption]
Decoction for gargling, powder licked with honey for cough, or boiled in milk for ulcers.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
Can cause fluid retention and elevate blood pressure if consumed in large quantities over a long period. Deglycyrrhizinated forms (DGL) are safer for long-term GI use.
