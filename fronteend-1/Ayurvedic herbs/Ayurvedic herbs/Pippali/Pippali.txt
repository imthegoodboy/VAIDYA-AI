--- PIPPALI ---

[Intro]
Pippali (Long Pepper) is a unique heating spice that acts as a potent rejuvenator (Rasayana) for the respiratory system.

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Order: Piperales | Family: Piperaceae | Genus: Piper | Species: P. longum

[Morphology]
A slender aromatic climber with heart-shaped dark green leaves. The fruits (spikes) are cylindrical, turning from green to black when dried.

[Habitat and Distribution]
Native to the hotter parts of India, including the lower hills of Bengal, the evergreen forests of the Western Ghats, and the Himalayas.

[Historical Background]
Historically highly traded alongside black pepper. It is a key ingredient in 'Trikatu', the classic Ayurvedic digestive blend.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Piperine, piplartine, essential oils, and volatile compounds.

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Bioenhancer (increases absorption of other nutrients/herbs), treats asthma, clears mucus, detoxifies the liver, and ignites digestive fire.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa: Pungent | Virya: Heating | Vipaka: Sweet | Dosha: Pacifies Vata and Kapha.

[Methods of Consumption]
Powdered fruit consumed with honey for coughs, or boiled in milk. Always used in small quantities.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
Due to its strong heating nature, prolonged excessive use without a cooling medium (like ghee/milk) can aggravate Pitta and cause acidity.
