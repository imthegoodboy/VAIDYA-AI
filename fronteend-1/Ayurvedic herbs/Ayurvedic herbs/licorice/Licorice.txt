--- LICORICE ---

[Intro]
Licorice (also known as Yashtimadhu) is a sweet, soothing root used globally for digestive, respiratory, and adrenal support.

[Taxonomical Classification]
Kingdom: Plantae | Order: Fabales | Family: Fabaceae | Genus: Glycyrrhiza | Species: G. glabra

[Morphology]
A herbaceous perennial growing up to 1 meter tall, with pinnate leaves and pale blue/violet flowers. The roots are stoloniferous and woody.

[Habitat and Distribution]
Native to Western Asia, North Africa, and Southern Europe. Thrives in well-drained soils in deep valleys.

[Historical Background]
Used by ancient Egyptians, Greeks, and in Chinese and Ayurvedic medicine to soothe coughs and digestive ulcers.

[Phytochemical Constituents]
Glycyrrhizin (50 times sweeter than sugar), flavonoids (liquiritin), glabridin, and various phytoestrogens.

[Scientific and Medicinal Benefits]
Soothes acid reflux, helps heal peptic ulcers, acts as a potent expectorant for coughs, supports adrenal fatigue, and has antiviral properties.

[Ayurvedic Perspective]
Rasa: Sweet | Virya: Cooling | Vipaka: Sweet | Dosha: Balances Vata and Pitta; increases Kapha if used excessively.

[Methods of Consumption]
Decoction (tea), powder mixed with milk or honey, lozenges, and standardized deglycyrrhizinated licorice (DGL) tablets.

[Side Effects and Precautions]
High doses of glycyrrhizin can cause potassium depletion and high blood pressure (pseudoaldosteronism). Use DGL to avoid cardiovascular effects.
