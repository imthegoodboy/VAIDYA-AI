1. Introduction

Neem, scientifically known as Azadirachta indica, is a medicinal tree widely used in Ayurveda. Known for its bitter taste and powerful antibacterial properties, neem is commonly used for skin care, blood purification, and oral hygiene.

2. Taxonomical Classification
Category	Classification
Kingdom	Plantae
Family	Meliaceae
Genus	Azadirachta
Species	Azadirachta indica
3. Morphology
Stem and Bark
Tall evergreen tree with rough brown bark
Can grow up to 15–20 meters tall
Leaves
Compound green leaves with serrated edges
Bitter in taste
Flowers
Small white fragrant flowers
Bloom in clusters
Fruits
Smooth oval-shaped yellow-green fruits
Contain a single seed
4. Historical Background
Used in Ayurveda for thousands of years
Traditionally called the “Village Pharmacy” in India
Commonly used for:
Treating skin infections
Dental care
Purifying blood
Protecting against infections
5. Scientific Benefits
Antibacterial Properties

Contains natural compounds that help fight bacteria and infections.

Skin Health

Used for acne, eczema, and other skin disorders because of its cleansing properties.

Blood Purification

Traditionally believed to help detoxify and purify the blood.

Oral Hygiene

Neem twigs and extracts are used to maintain healthy teeth and gums.

Anti-inflammatory Effects

May help reduce inflammation and support wound healing.

6. Ayurvedic Perspective
Primarily balances Pitta dosha
Considered cooling and detoxifying
Used to cleanse the body and skin
7. Common Uses
5

Neem is commonly used as:

8. Precautions

Excessive use may cause:

Stomach irritation
Nausea
Dryness in some individuals

Pregnant women and small children should avoid excessive medicinal use without medical advice.
