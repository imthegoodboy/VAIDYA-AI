1. Introduction

Garlic, scientifically known as Allium sativum, is a medicinal and culinary herb widely used in Ayurveda. It is valued as a powerful Rasayana for supporting heart health, improving circulation, fighting infections, and enhancing digestion.

2. Taxonomical Classification
Category	Classification
Kingdom	Plantae
Family	Amaryllidaceae
Genus	Allium
Species	Allium sativum
3. Morphology
Bulb
Underground bulb made of multiple cloves
Strong pungent aroma
Main medicinally used part
Leaves
Long narrow green leaves
Flat and soft texture
Flowers
Small white or pale pink flowers
Grow in rounded clusters
Plant Height
Usually grows up to 30–60 cm tall
4. Historical Background
Used in Ayurveda and traditional medicine for thousands of years
Traditionally used for:
Improving heart health
Treating respiratory conditions
Fighting infections
Enhancing strength and digestion
5. Scientific Benefits
Heart and Circulatory Support

May help support healthy blood circulation and reduce LDL cholesterol levels.

Antimicrobial Properties

Contains compounds like allicin that help fight bacteria, viruses, and fungi.

Respiratory Support

Traditionally used for cough, congestion, and respiratory infections.

Digestive Enhancement

Helps stimulate digestion and improve metabolic activity.

Antioxidant Activity

Protects body cells from oxidative stress and damage.

6. Ayurvedic Perspective
Balances Vata and Kapha doshas
Considered warming and stimulating
Used to improve circulation, digestion, and respiratory health

8. Precautions

Excessive use may cause:

Acidity
Stomach irritation
Strong body odor

People taking blood-thinning medications should consult a healthcare professional before regular medicinal use.
